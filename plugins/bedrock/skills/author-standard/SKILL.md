# Author a Standard

A standard is a reusable artifact that governs future work. This skill decides whether it should exist, establishes its authority and binding, and requires proportionate proof before adoption.

## Interaction contract

- **Inputs:** a recognizable repeated task, durable authority sources, the intended binding, and representative consumers.
- **Output:** a scoped standard with provenance, exceptions, proving criteria, and lifecycle metadata.
- **Authority:** the author may define guidance within the declared binding; the operator owns adoption and material exceptions.
- **Capabilities:** durable source access and one representative consumer are required. Generated carriers and drift checks are optional but must be declared when used.
- **Failure:** do not publish when membership, authority, binding, provenance, or proving evidence is missing.
- **Evidence:** source grading, counterexamples, consumer results, and an explicit success threshold.
- **Lifecycle:** proposed → proving → adopted → revised → deprecated, superseded, rolled back, or retired. See [lifecycle and provenance](reference/lifecycle-and-provenance.md).

## Membership and scope

A skill is a task-triggered unit. If no recognizable request should invoke it, the rule belongs in always-loaded context or a smaller artifact. Keep one clean job per skill and choose the smallest artifact that carries the value.

Declare the binding—stack, toolchain, audience, authority corpus, and runtime assumptions. A configuration already represented by a profile is not a rebind. A rebind must state preserved invariants, changed axes, replacement authority, migration, exceptions, and proving evidence.

## Source and provenance

Author from established authority plus exercised judgment. Operational systems, incidents, and successful code are admissible evidence when triangulated, provenance-bearing, reviewed, and accompanied by counterevidence; a single uncontrolled output is not canon.

Every normative claim has section- or claim-level provenance: source identity, version or access date, evidence type and grade, applicability, conflicts, and the author's explicit judgment where sources do not settle the rule. Unsupported authority language is a defect, not a stylistic omission.

## Inline or reference

“Point, do not mirror” is not absolute. Decide whether content is inline, referenced, or generated by evaluating:

- availability to the runtime and consumer;
- single-authority and drift risk;
- size and context cost;
- update cadence and licensing; and
- whether parity can be checked mechanically.

Inline a compact load-bearing rule when the consumer cannot reliably fetch it. Reference a durable available authority when it can. Use generated carriers for necessary runtime duplication and record source identity and digest. Hand-maintained duplication without a drift control is non-conforming.

## Exceptions

Every exception records owner, scope, rationale, risk—including security impact—expiry or review trigger, and evidence. A site exception is local and bounded. A record exception changes what downstream work may assume and requires a decision record. Repeated exceptions are aggregated as evidence that the standard may be wrong or its binding too broad.

## Prove it

Declare a risk-proportionate proving profile before adoption. At minimum name:

- positive and negative task cases;
- a counterexample or adversarial case;
- the representative consumer;
- deterministic checks and judgment evaluations separately;
- success, failure, and unavailable-evidence thresholds; and
- retained evidence and its reproduction path.

When a consumer diverges, choose explicitly: fix the consumer, promote a validated missing rule, or retract an overclaim. Check the standard's rules for joint satisfiability before granting an exception.

## Boundaries

- Decision-record instances → `author-decision-record` (construct specs then route to `author-construct-spec`).
- Finished-change review → `code-review`.
- Mechanical packaging and routing infrastructure → `skill-creator` after this skill settles the standard.

## Distribution boundary

This skill requires adoption and proving evidence but does not make a private rollout ledger part of the installed contract. Repository-visible release and currency evidence belongs to package governance; operational consumer rollout may remain external. Until that capability exists, report distribution completeness as unverified rather than inferring it from a tracker.
